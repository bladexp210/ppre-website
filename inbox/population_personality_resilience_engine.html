Plain-English Statistical Glossary

Probability Density

In simple terms: The height of the curve shows how common or clustered a specific score is in the population. The higher the peak, the more people in that group have that exact score. The total area underneath each curve always equals 100% of that population.

Standard Deviation (SD or σ)

In simple terms: A measure of diversity or "spread" within the group. A small SD means almost everyone is clustered tightly around the average. A large SD means individuals are spread far and wide—some very low, some very high. In normal populations, roughly 68% of people fall within ±1 SD of the mean.

Interactive Score Inspector

In simple terms: A tool that lets you pick any score on the 1.0 to 5.0 scale (like 3.8 / High) and see what fraction of Group A versus Group B score at or above that threshold. It shows how small mean shifts can lead to noticeable differences at the extremes.

Top 20% Extreme Scores (The Room of 100)

In simple terms: If you take only the top one-fifth highest-scoring individuals across the entire population, this grid shows what percentage belong to Cohort A versus Cohort B. Even when average differences are small, higher-scoring groups are more represented at extreme cut-offs.

Cohen's d (Standardized Effect Size)

In simple terms: The distance between the two group centers, measured in standard deviation steps.
• $d = 0.20$: Small shift (hard to notice in everyday life).
• $d = 0.50$: Moderate shift (visible to an observer).
• $d = 0.80+$: Large difference.

Distribution Overlap (OVL)

In simple terms: The percentage of area that both groups share in common. When overlap is 85%, only 15% of the curve represents differences between the groups. This proves that you cannot judge an individual person solely by their group's demographic average.

Common Language Effect Size (CLES)

In simple terms: The "coin-toss" probability. If you pull one random member from Group A and one from Group B, CLES is the likelihood that Person A has a higher score than Person B. If groups are identical, CLES is exactly 50%.

Scientific Framework & Provenance

1. Johnson's IPIP-NEO-120 ($N = 619,150$)

Derived from Dr. John A. Johnson's normative database (2014, Journal of Research in Personality). Measures 30 sub-facets with 4 items per facet. Reverse-scored using standard keying, trimmed for careless responding and straightlining.

2. UKHLS Longitudinal Panel ($N > 40,000$)

Administered by ISER (University of Essex) through the UK Data Service (SN 6614). Tracks adult life events alongside the 15-item BFI-S across multiple survey waves.

3. Metric Formulas

  • Cohen's d = (Mean_A - Mean_B) / sqrt((SD_A² + SD_B²) / 2)
  • Overlap % (OVL) = 2 * Φ(-|d| / 2) * 100%
  • CLES (Probability of Superiority) = Φ(d / sqrt(2)) * 100%
